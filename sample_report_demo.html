How the scores are built
  • Peers now: each valuation metric is converted to a yield (for example 1 ÷ P/E), ranked within the sector, and combined using the weights in config.py. Converting to yields keeps loss-makers in the ranking as expensive, rather than producing meaningless negative multiples.
  • Own history: at each quarter end over the last 4 years, the company’s yield minus the sector median gives a spread. Today’s spread is ranked against the company’s past spreads. This removes market-wide re-rating. The fundamentals only change once a year, so treat this as indicative: the quarterly points are not independent.
  • Dividend yield history: today’s trailing 12-month yield ranked against up to 15 years of daily history, with special dividends excluded.
  • Quality: sector percentiles of profitability, cash and balance-sheet measures. For net debt/EBITDA and share count change, lower ranks higher.
  • Multiples or yields: the switch in the sidebar changes how valuation metrics are displayed (P/E of 10.0x or earnings yield of 10.0%). Scores are always calculated on yields, so they don’t change. Loss-makers show as n/m in multiple view and as a negative yield in yield view.
  • Forward P/E comes from Yahoo’s current analyst-estimate snapshot. It counts towards the peer score but has no history.
  • Composite: weighted 45% peers, 25% own history, 10% dividend history, 20% quality. Missing components are dropped and the weights rescaled.
  • Accounts are assumed to become public 120 days after the period end, so historical scores only use information available at the time.
